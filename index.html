🐾 Meet Our Animals

Get to know the amazing residents of Cedar Valley Zoo. From our colourful aviary to our reptile residents, our collection will grow as we build new habitats!

Toco Toucan

The Toco Toucan is famous for its enormous orange beak. Despite its size, the beak is surprisingly lightweight. This bird lives in South America and spends much of its time among the trees.

Its long beak helps it reach fruit, and it plays an important role in spreading seeds through tropical environments.

Habitat: Tropical forest

Diet: Mainly fruit

Our Aviary

Javan Green Magpie

Native to the forests of Java, Indonesia, the Javan Green Magpie is known for its vivid green plumage and striking dark facial markings.

These intelligent members of the crow family benefit from complex habitats where they can explore, perch and search for food.

Habitat: Tropical forest

Diet: Invertebrates and small animals

Our Aviary

Long-tailed Parakeet

The Long-tailed Parakeet is a colourful parrot from Southeast Asia. Its long tail gives it an elegant shape as it moves between branches.

Active and curious, these birds benefit from space to climb, perch and explore their surroundings.

Habitat: Forest and woodland

Diet: Fruit, seeds and plant matter

Our Aviary

Great Hornbill

The Great Hornbill is a magnificent forest bird with a huge curved bill and a distinctive casque on top. It lives in parts of South and Southeast Asia.

Great Hornbills help forests regenerate by spreading seeds after eating fruit. They are an important part of their forest ecosystems.

Habitat: Tropical forest

Diet: Mainly fruit, plus small animals

Our Aviary

Hawksbill Sea Turtle

The Hawksbill Sea Turtle is a beautiful marine reptile named for its narrow, pointed beak, which resembles a hawk's bill. Its shell has overlapping scales and patterns of amber, brown and gold.

Hawksbill Sea Turtles are especially associated with tropical coral reefs. Their narrow beaks help them reach food hidden in reef crevices. They feed mainly on sponges, helping maintain the balance of coral reef ecosystems.

These turtles travel through warm ocean waters and return to beaches to lay their eggs. Protecting nesting beaches, coral reefs and ocean habitats is essential for their future.

Habitat: Tropical oceans and coral reefs

Diet: Mainly marine sponges

Conservation: Critically Endangered

Our Aquarium

Moon Jelly

The Moon Jelly is a graceful jellyfish known for its translucent, saucer-shaped bell. Four horseshoe-shaped structures can often be seen through the top of its delicate body.

Moon Jellies drift through the water and pulse their bells to help them move. Their bodies are mostly made of water, giving them their delicate, almost see-through appearance.

These jellyfish feed on tiny drifting animals, plankton and other small food particles. They use their short tentacles and other structures to capture food from the water.

Moon Jellies can be found in coastal waters in many parts of the world. They are fascinating animals that show how diverse marine life can be.

Habitat: Coastal seas and ocean waters

Diet: Plankton and tiny marine animals

Animal group: Jellyfish

Komodo Dragon

The Komodo Dragon is the largest living lizard in the world! This impressive reptile is native to several Indonesian islands.

It has a powerful body, strong legs, sharp claws and a long tail. Its forked tongue helps it detect scents in its surroundings.

Komodo Dragons are carnivores. They hunt and scavenge for food, using their powerful jaws and sharp, serrated teeth.

These remarkable reptiles face threats from habitat changes and other environmental pressures. Protecting their natural homes is important for their future.

Habitat: Tropical dry forests and savannas

Diet: Carnivore

Native range: Indonesian islands

Conservation: Endangered

🐠 Clown Anemonefish

The Clown Anemonefish is a bright reef fish recognised by its orange body and distinctive white bands outlined in black.

It lives among sea anemones, whose stinging tentacles provide protection. In return, the fish can help keep its host anemone clean and may defend it from certain threats.

Clown Anemonefish are fascinating examples of cooperation between different species in tropical coral reef ecosystems.

Habitat: Tropical coral reefs

Diet: Small invertebrates and algae

Native range: Indo-Pacific region

Reef Fish NEW RESIDENT!

💙 Blue Tang

The Blue Tang is a striking reef fish with a vivid blue body, dark markings and a bright yellow tail. Its colourful appearance makes it a favourite among many ocean enthusiasts.

Blue Tangs are active swimmers that explore coral reefs in search of food. They feed mainly on algae, helping control algal growth and contributing to the health of reef habitats.

Coral reefs provide food, shelter and places for many marine species to live. Protecting these environments benefits Blue Tangs and countless other reef animals.

Habitat: Tropical coral reefs

Diet: Mainly algae

Native range: Indo-Pacific region

Reef Fish NEW RESIDENT!

🦁 Red Lionfish

The Red Lionfish is a spectacular marine fish with reddish-brown and white stripes, long fan-like fins and distinctive spines.

Its striking fins help it appear larger and can assist it when cornering prey. Its venomous spines provide defence against potential predators.

Red Lionfish are predators that feed on smaller fish and crustaceans. In parts of the western Atlantic and Caribbean where they are invasive, they can put pressure on native reef wildlife.

Lionfish must be handled carefully because their venomous spines can cause painful injuries.

Habitat: Coral reefs and rocky marine habitats

Diet: Small fish and crustaceans

Native range: Indo-Pacific region

🐠 Protecting Our Oceans

Coral reefs support an incredible diversity of life. Pollution, climate change and damage to reef habitats threaten marine ecosystems around the world.

At Cedar Valley Zoo, our aquarium celebrates the fascinating animals that live in our oceans and encourages everyone to care for the underwater world.
